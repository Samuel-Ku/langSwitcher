---
title: 'Thought Recovery'
description: 'Recover sentences that mix languages and wrong keyboard layouts, span by span, without touching code, links or intentional foreign words.'
---

Thought Recovery reconstructs a whole line that mixes languages and keyboard layouts, instead of converting one wrong-layout tail. It is the macOS port of the [Thought Recovery idea](https://github.com/Samuel-Ku/langswitcher-linux/blob/main/docs/ideas/thought-recovery.md) from the Linux port.

The line you actually type:

```
ghbdsn, я вже зробив rjvsn але tests gflf.nm
```

What Thought Recovery makes of it:

```
привіт, я вже зробив коміт але tests падають
```

Four things changed — the Ukrainian words typed on the U.S. layout are back — and `tests`, which you meant to type in English, is untouched.

## Enabling It

- **Settings → General → Thought Recovery** — turns span recovery on or off (on by default).
- **Show Preview Before Applying** — show the reconstruction and the alternatives, and apply it by hand (off by default).
- **Menu bar → Preview Recovery…** — recover the line around the cursor and always show the preview.

When preview is off, the hotkey flow replaces the line directly, exactly like Greedy Line conversion but span by span. Press the hotkey again to convert the result back.

## What It Never Touches

Automatic rewriting only happens when the evidence for a span is decisive. These are always left alone:

- **Code** — `user_id`, `maxRetries`, `SCREAMING_CASE`, numbers, anything with underscores or digits.
- **Links and e-mail** — `https://github.com/reg2005/langSwitcher`, `test@example.com`, known domains.
- **Intentional foreign words** — `pull request`, `main`, `deploy`, English words inside a Ukrainian sentence.
- **Correct text** — a sentence already in the right layout comes back character for character.
- **Single characters**, with one exception. A lone lowercase Latin letter in a Cyrillic sentence is rewritten only when its wrong-layout reading is a real word of that language (`z` → `я`, `s` → `і`). An uppercase single letter (a size, an option — `розмір S`) and a lone letter in Latin text (`z` in Polish "z tego") are never touched.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-info" color="blue"}
Punctuation that types a letter on a PC layout counts as a letter only **between two letters**: the `.` key types `ю`, the `,` key types `б` and the `;` key types `ж`. So `gflf.nm` recovers to `падають`, `cghj,eq` to `спробуй` and `d;t` to `вже`. The same character at the end of a word is real punctuation, so `ghbdtn,` recovers to `привет,` and not to `приветб`.
::

## Reading the Preview

The preview shows the original line, the reconstruction, up to **two alternative readings** for ambiguous text, and every changed span with its segment type, its margin and the overall confidence.

| Segment type | Meaning |
|--------------|---------|
| `wrongLayout` | Typed on the wrong layout and rewritten |
| `intentionalForeign` | A real word in another language, kept as it is |
| `code` | Identifier, snippet or number — untouched |
| `url` | Link, domain or e-mail — untouched |
| `properName` | Name or brand, kept as it is |
| `ambiguous` | Not enough evidence, original kept |
| `correct` | Already right |
| `separator` | Punctuation and whitespace |

## Undo Teaches the Personal Dictionary

**Keep original** in the preview restores the original text and teaches every word of it to a personal dictionary. Those words are never rewritten again — by Thought Recovery, by the Space auto-correction, or by any other conversion path. The dictionary holds up to 5000 words, is stored in your user defaults, and never leaves your Mac.

Settings → General also tracks how often a recovery was accepted and reverted, so you can see whether the decoder is pulling its weight.

## How It Decides

1. The line is split into word, whitespace and punctuation runs; code, URLs and e-mails are classified hard.
2. Adjacent tokens may merge, because punctuation glued between two letters of a wrong-layout word is a letter.
3. Every span gets candidates: keep the text, or convert it between the enabled layouts (including the ⌥-layer chords used for Polish diacritics on a Cyrillic layout).
4. Candidates are scored with the embedded dictionaries, character n-grams and a vowel/consonant structure factor that flags impossible letter runs such as `ghbdsn`.
5. A first pass finds the language that carries the sentence, a second pass re-scores with that prior — this is what breaks ties such as a lone `z`.
6. A beam search over segmentations, penalising language switches and needless splits, picks the best reading.
7. A span is rewritten only when its best candidate beats the runner-up by a minimum margin. Everything else keeps the original text and is reported as ambiguous.

No language model, no network access. The dictionaries and n-grams ship inside the app.

## Limitations

- Mixed sentences work best for **EN/UK**. Other pairs (PL, RU, DE, FR, ES) work through the same layout machinery but have no dedicated corpus.
- A lone letter is rewritten only in the narrow wrong-layout case described above; every other single character keeps its text.
- The decoder is measured against a generated corpus of mixed sentences (whole-sentence, single-word and scattered wrong-layout corruption) plus correct lines that must not change. Turn on the preview if the automatic reading is ever wrong — keeping the original then also teaches it.
- Recovering a line shows as `recover` in the conversion log.
